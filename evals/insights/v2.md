**Fixed costs vs per-byte costs.**
- **Fixed:** spawn, plus serial lookups (0.105 s, growing with the link count), plus the tail. That's 0.19 s at 20M on macOS and 0.25 s on Linux.
- **The tail isn't really fixed.** It's proportional to the number of resident 4 KB pages, so it's a per-byte cost paid serially at exit.
- **At the target scale the serial part dominates.** Lookups plus tail would be 0.11 s of a 0.25 s budget. Both need to go to ≈ 0.02 s each, and serial lookups are now the #2 fixed cost after the tail.

### 4.2 Page faults: what they cost and how to remove them [probe]

**Where the 811k faults come from.** I sampled `/proc/<pid>/smaps_rollup` every 0.1 s inside the container (THP mode `always`, the Docker VM default):

| t (s into run) | minflt | Rss | Anonymous | AnonHugePages |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.2 | 57k | 444 MB | 248 MB | 68 MB |
| 0.9 | 393k | 2,709 MB | 1,531 MB | 68 MB |
| 1.4 | 591k | 4,020 MB | 2,287 MB | 68 MB |
| end | 815k | 5,416 MB | 3,587 MB | 500 MB |

- **About 97% are anonymous 4 KB faults.** (3,587 − 500) MB ÷ 4 KB ≈ 790k.
- **The mmapped input costs only ≈ 25k faults.** Fault-around maps 64 KB per fault, so `MAP_POPULATE` or pre-touching the input buys little on a warm run.
- **THP covers only 0.5 GB of 3.6 GB, even with THP `always`.** `thp_fault_fallback` is 0, so the kernel never even tried.
- **The cause is glibc malloc.** v2's (and v3's) many per-chunk × per-bucket `Vec`s (≈ 17 KB each in v2) are served from glibc's per-thread arenas. Those grow their heaps a little at a time with `mprotect`/`brk`, so no 2 MB-aligned range is ever fully inside the writable VMA when the fault happens. The kernel falls back to 4 KB pages without counting a fallback.
- **Under THP `madvise`** (the Ubuntu and Amazon Linux default, and the most likely scorer setting), glibc takes **970k** faults.

**The fix is a global allocator swap.** The probe added three lines: `mimalloc = "0.1"` plus `#[global_allocator]`. Runs below are seed-42, 8 CPUs, 22 GiB, interleaved, process wall from `/usr/bin/time`:

| THP mode | Allocator | Runs (s) | Median | minflt | sys (s) | user (s) | tail (wall − last mark) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| always | glibc (v2 as shipped) | 1.87, 1.98, 2.06 | **1.98** | 816k | 1.05 | 12.2 | 0.13 |
| always | mimalloc | 1.67, 1.60, 1.63 | **1.63** | 22k | 0.14 | 11.7 | ≈ 0.005 |
| always | mimalloc + `MIMALLOC_ALLOW_LARGE_OS_PAGES=1` | 1.58, 1.55, 1.59 | **1.58** | 21k | 0.12 | 11.4 | ≈ 0.005 |
| madvise | glibc | 2.08, 2.12 | **2.10** | 971k | 1.11 | 12.9 | 0.15 |
| madvise | mimalloc | 1.57, 1.56 | **1.565** | 20k | 0.13 | 11.4 | ≈ 0.005 |
| never | glibc | 1.98, 1.97 | **1.975** | 966k | 1.08 | 12.0 | 0.15 |
| never | mimalloc | 1.83, 1.81 | **1.82** | 901k | 0.88 | 11.6 | 0.11 |

`glibc.malloc.hugetlb=1` (the glibc 2.36 tunable) changed nothing: 817k faults, and 1.95 s against 2.06 to 2.19 s is within noise.

Reading the table:
- **With THP `madvise` or `always`, mimalloc removes 98% of the faults and 0.35 to 0.55 s of wall at 20M (−18 to −25%)**. That makes Linux *faster* than macOS native.
  - mimalloc reserves large, aligned segments and asks for THP on them itself, so it gets 2 MB pages even under `madvise`.
  - The gain has three parts: sys time (−0.9 core-s), user time (−0.5 to −1.5 core-s from fewer TLB misses, which matters doubly under nested paging in a VM), and the exit tail (0.13 → 0.005 s, because there are 512× fewer PTEs to tear down).
- **With THP `never`**, mimalloc still gains −0.15 s (−8%) from faster allocation and reuse.
- **RSS rises about 5%** (5.55 → 5.85 GB at 20M). At 60M that means roughly +0.6 GB. It's fine against the gate, but Eval should confirm it.

**What faults cost on the scorer (estimate).** On a Xeon cloud VM (EPT), a 4 KB anonymous fault costs about 0.5 to 1 µs including zeroing; the Docker VM here runs ≈ 1.1 µs/fault in sys alone. For v2 at 20M:
- 0.8 to 1.0M faults × 0.5 to 1 µs ≈ 0.5 to 1 vCPU-s, or **0.1 to 0.2 s wall**;
- plus a 0.13 to 0.15 s serial exit tail;
- plus the TLB penalty: **≈ 0.25 to 0.4 s of v2's scorer time in total, and ≈ 3× that at 60M.**

For the target: a 0.28 s run on 8 vCPUs has ≈ 2.2 vCPU-s, and 800k faults would eat about a third of it. **Any design that wins must either touch less than ≈ 0.5 GB of fresh 4 KB memory or get it on huge pages.** The leaders almost certainly do one or the other.

**Ranked ways to remove faults:**
1. mimalloc (measured above: done in 3 lines).
2. Fewer and larger allocations (v3's compact rows are 32 B against 80 B, which roughly halves the footprint).
3. Explicit `mmap` arenas with `madvise(MADV_HUGEPAGE)` for the 2 to 3 biggest buffers (the row arena and the link table). Only worth it if mimalloc is rejected.
4. Reuse: drop the event arena before allocating the session arrays, so the allocator recycles pages instead of faulting new ones. mimalloc does this automatically.
5. Pre-faulting in parallel (`MAP_POPULATE`) only moves the cost, and the input is already cheap. Don't bother.

### 4.3 What the < 0.25 s architecture needs (update to v1 §4.3)

v1's shape still holds: one fused pass, SIMD structural scan, packed IDs, one shuffle, dedup by side stream, batched lookups. Two additions from this round:
- **Huge-page-backed memory is a hard requirement**, not a polish item (§4.2). With mimalloc that's free.
- **Serial work must be ≤ 0.03 s in total.** That means parallel link parsing and interning (the link phase is 0.105 s, the biggest serial cost left) and a near-zero tail, which THP pages give.

Updated local budget for v3 plus mimalloc plus SIMD at 20M: parse ≈ 1.0 core-s, clicks 0.2, dedup 0.15, sessions 0.35, write 0.05 → ≈ 1.75 core-s ÷ 6.4 ≈ 0.27 s, plus ≈ 0.04 s serial. **≈ 0.30 s local, and on Linux about the same rather than 25% worse.**

## 7. Confidence and open questions

- **High confidence:**
  - Glibc's 4 KB anonymous faults cost v2 ≈ 0.35 to 0.5 s at 20M in the Linux VM, and mimalloc removes them. That's 3 interleaved runs per arm under `always` and 2 under `madvise`/`never`, with the gaps far larger than the run-to-run spread.
  - The teardown shortfall is kernel `exit_mmap` time and scales with the 4 KB page count.
- **Medium confidence:**
  - The size of the gain on the scorer. A Xeon VM with EPT probably has cheaper faults than Docker Desktop's nested setup, but also pays more for TLB misses (Cascade Lake STLB against M5).
  - 60M behaviour with mimalloc (RSS +5% extrapolated, not measured).
  - The v3 projection (0.6 to 0.7 s), which is still from v1-era probes.
- **Open questions:**
  1. The scorer's THP mode. mimalloc wins under `madvise` and `always` (−18 to −25%) and still helps under `never` (−8%), so the move is robust to it.
  2. The local→scorer ratio. There are still no data points; see §1.
  3. Whether the scorer's 8 vCPUs are 4 cores with HT. If so, the compute-bound moves (2, 4) are worth more than the probe suggests, because HT siblings share the vector units.
  4. Whether macOS native changes with mimalloc. Not measured (the machine was reserved for Linux probes); Method should check it.

## Appendix: probe method (bench lock held; ≈ 6 min total)

- Image `sessions-eval:953af436f8aa-arm64` plus GNU `time` (as in `linux_proxy.py`). Variant image: the same source at 953af43 plus `mimalloc = "0.1"` and a `#[global_allocator]`, built from the repo Dockerfile (scratchpad copy only; nothing committed).
- seed-42 was copied into a named volume. Run flags: `--read-only --cpus 8 --memory 22g --user 1000:1000 --network none`, tmpfs output, scratch and `/tmp`, and `NCPU=8`. Every run was warm (the volume had been read once before). Variants were interleaved.
- THP mode was switched with `docker run --privileged ... echo <mode> > /sys/kernel/mm/transparent_hugepage/enabled` and restored to `always` afterwards (verified). Kernel 7.0.14-linuxkit, glibc 2.36, defrag `madvise`, 4 KB base pages.
- Fault anatomy: a background `/app/sessions` with a 0.1 s loop reading `/proc/$pid/smaps_rollup` and `/proc/$pid/stat` field 10, plus `/proc/vmstat` deltas (`pgfault`, `thp_fault_alloc`, `thp_fault_fallback`).
- The volume and lock were removed after the runs.
