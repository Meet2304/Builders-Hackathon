## 2. What went well (metrics)

- **Correctness is airtight for the method's scope.** 36/36 ×3, dev ×3, 10/10 diff (including 20M seed-42 and Feb 29 2028
  seeds 15 and 62), and fuzz 842/842 strict plus 158/158 robust over 1000 seeds with NCPU cycling 1/2/4/8. The fuzz
  suite kills 37/37 mutants. That gives us a safety net for an aggressive rewrite.
- **The parallel phases scale near the hardware limit.** Per-phase speedups at 1→8 threads: events parse 6.3×,
  sessions 5.9×, clicks 5.7×, dedup 5.4×. The 8 threads run on **5 P-cores + 3 E-cores** (the host is 5P+10E). An
  E-core is about 0.45 of a P-core, so the hardware ceiling is about 6.4×. Parallel parse is at **≈ 98 % of what this
  machine can give**.
- **No idle wait.** CPU utilisation is 0.85 at 20M, and the missing 15 % equals the serial phases (lookups 0.095 s +
  teardown 0.110 s = 13 % of the wall). There's no lock contention: 6 voluntary context switches per run.
- **Very stable measurements.** The 20M CV is 2 %. Instructions are 91.8 G ± 0.1 % across runs.
- **Write is cheap** (0.043 s, 3 %). Thread count is 9 and stdout is 0 bytes. The binary is 3.8 MB and the image 120 to 142 MB.
- **No load-imbalance risk.** The largest person holds 1.84 % of surviving events at 20M (1.70 % at 60M), from the
  oracle output, and the top 5 hold 7 %. Partitioning by person hash with ≥ 64 partitions can't be bottlenecked by a whale.

## 3. What failed or regressed

- **Speed: 5.5× off the target, and the cause is raw CPU work, not parallelism.** At 20M, v1 burns **9.77 s user
  (8.37 s single-threaded)**, which is **4,270 instructions and ≈ 2,090 cycles per input event row** (IPC 2.04). A
  0.25 s run on 8 cores allows about 1.6 core-s in total, so **we need about 5× less CPU work, not better scaling.**
- **60M is superlinear by +25 %** (90 ns/event against 72 ns at 20M). Instructions per row stay flat (4,412 against 4,270),
  but **IPC drops from 2.04 to 1.75**: memory stalls. Three causes:
  - the slice-keyed link table grows from 1.2M to 3.6M IDs, and each probe dereferences key bytes in the mmap, so
    there are 2 to 3 dependent misses per row;
  - `NB=256` is fixed, so a dedup bucket at 60M is about 250k × 80 B = 20 MB, which falls out of cache (dedup grows
    5.2× for 3× the data);
  - sys time grows 4.7× (0.77 s to 3.61 s) from page faults on 11.5 GB of anonymous memory.
- **Memory is 4 to 6× larger than needed.** The `Ev` row is 80 B and holds two 16 B slices. Then:
  - every row is copied three times (per-chunk bucket Vec, then `scatter`, then `PEv` + `scatter` again);
  - the Vec doubling of 400 chunks × 256 buckets over-allocates by up to 2×.

  That's a 4.1 GB footprint at 20M and 11.5 GB at 60M. **This is our biggest scorer-specific risk.** On Linux with
  4 KB pages, first-touch of fresh anonymous memory costs about 0.1 s/GB single-threaded **[probe: Docker VM, 11.1 GB/s
  first touch against 22.5 GB/s re-touch; THP brings it to 50 GB/s]**. That's ≈ 0.4 core-s at 20M and ≈ 1.2 core-s at
  60M that the M5 never sees.
- **Fixed costs are 13 % of the wall, all serial:**
  - teardown is **0.110 s at 20M and 0.354 s at 60M**. The "after last mark" time doesn't fall with threads (0.103 s
    at NCPU=1): it's `main` dropping ~4 GB of Vecs plus munmapping the input;
  - lookups and links run single-threaded: 0.095 s at 20M and 0.323 s at 60M.
- **Noise:** none flagged. One caveat is that the 20M samples are bimodal (1.547 to 1.550 against 1.598 to 1.609). That
  looks like P/E scheduling luck and will matter once runs are under 0.4 s.

### 4.2 Per-row cost decomposition [probe]

This is a stripped prototype of a fused events parse (packed u64 IDs, 32 B rows, one shuffle; fast path only, not
exact; scratchpad, never shipped). Run single-threaded on seed-42 (21.5M rows), with stages enabled cumulatively:

| Stage | Cumulative s | Δ ns/row | Comment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| mmap + memchr line iteration | 0.41 | 15 | includes 116k page faults on the input |
| + byte-loop 7-field split (v1's algorithm) | 1.29 | **41** | 680 instructions/row. **SIMD target** |
| + pack event_id + parse `ingest_ts` | 1.78 | 23 | SWAR the fixed F1 form |
| + `ts`, window, type, price, product → category | 2.10 | 15 | |
| + emit (eid, ingest) 16 B to an eid partition | 2.09 | ~0 | streaming writes are free |
| + pack user_id | 2.43 | 16 | SWAR the pack |
| + link lookup (1.2M-key `HashMap<u64,u64>`) | 3.23 | **37** | one random DRAM miss per row. **Prefetch target** |
| + emit 32 B row to a person partition | 3.30 | 3 | |

At 8 threads the prototype takes **0.50 s** for events parse, against v1's 0.686 s. **Compact rows alone buy only
about 25 %.** The split and the link miss make up 60 % of what's left. The two levers that cut CPU work are
**SIMD structural scanning** and **batched, prefetched lookups**.

Floors [probe, 8 threads, 1.9 GB, warm cache]:
- touch every page: 0.032 s;
- `memchr` every `\n`: 0.036 s (51 GB/s);
- line split plus xxh3 of the first field: 0.060 s;
- `read()` into fresh buffers: 0.045 s;
- the `memchr2` iterator over every `,` and `\n` (171M hits): **0.152 s**, because per-match iterator overhead is ≈ 6 ns.
  A u64-bitmask scan (simdcsv style: compare 64 B against `,` `\n` `"`, then `tzcnt` the mask) avoids it.

### 4.3 What a < 0.25 s / 20M architecture looks like on 8 Xeon vCPUs

Leaders: 1.9 GB in 0.28 s ≈ 6.8 GB/s end to end. With 4C/8T that's ≈ 1.3 Xeon core-s, or **≈ 50 ns of core time per input
row** (events + clicks, 26.6M rows). v1 spends ≈ 370 ns per row (9.77 s of user time), about 7× more. That allows no second
pass over the CSV, no per-row heap traffic, and only about one cache miss per row. The shape that fits that budget:

1. **Input:** mmap each file. On Linux, `MAP_POPULATE`, or `madvise(MADV_WILLNEED/POPULATE_READ)` per worker range,
   pays the faults in parallel up front. Don't munmap.
2. **One fused pass per 4 MB chunk (rayon, ≥ 4 × NCPU chunks):** a 64-byte SIMD classifier gives `,` `\n` `"` bitmasks
   (AVX-512BW on the scorer, NEON locally, a portable u64 SWAR fallback otherwise). Iterate fields with `tzcnt`.
   - **Fast path:** a line with no `"` bit and exactly 6 commas. Otherwise fall back to v1's exact `split_fields`. That
     keeps correctness: the fast path only ever accepts lines the slow path would parse identically.
   - Timestamps get a SWAR fast path for the fixed F1 (`ingest_ts` is always F1) and F2 forms, with v1's `parse_ts` as
     the fallback for anything else.
3. **Packed, order-preserving IDs:** event_id, user_id and click_id are ≤ 9 bytes of `[0-9a-z]` in the generator's
   data **[probe]**.
   - Encode up to 10 chars × 6 bits, **left-aligned**, with symbols in ASCII order (`0-9` < `A-Z` < `_` < `a-z`) and 0 as
     padding, so that **u64 comparison equals byte-wise comparison** and the encoding is injective.
   - Anything else (quotes already stripped; spaces, UTF-8, > 10 chars) takes a tagged fallback: top bit set, plus an
     index into a side intern table. That path is rare and compares by bytes.
   - Person names need only one decode, at output time.
4. **Products:** `p<digits>` becomes a direct array index into a `Vec<u8>` of categories (50k entries, 50 KB, stays in
   L1/L2), with a hash fallback for anything else. Campaigns `c_<digits>` work the same way.
5. **Links:** parallel parse into packed pairs, then interning with sharded u64 tables, then union-find on u32, then the
   min packed key per component. Result: an open-addressing table `u64 user → u32 person` (plus `u64 name_key` per
   person). At 60M that's 3.6M IDs, ≈ 64 MB at load 0.5.
   - **Lookups are done in batches of 16 to 32 rows:** hash, then prefetch, then probe. That hides DRAM latency (37 →
     ~10 ns/row).
   - Use 2 MB pages (`MADV_HUGEPAGE`) on this table to kill TLB misses at 60M.
6. **Emit two compact streams per worker, into per-(worker, partition) arenas sized from the file bytes. No Vec growth.**
   - **eid stream:** `(eid u64, ingest_s u32, person_partition u16 | INVALID)` = 16 B for every parsed row with a
     non-empty event_id, including invalid rows, because dedup comes before validation. `ingest_ts` is whole-second F1,
     so u32 seconds since 2000 is exact. An unparsable ingest maps to u32::MAX.
   - **row stream:** `(person u32 or u64, ts_off u32 = ts − window_start in ms, price:24|cat:8 u32, eid u64, ingest_s u32)`
     = 24 to 32 B, for valid, non-late rows only, written to `partition = hash(person)`.
   - P = 256 at 20M; **scale P with input bytes** (P ≈ bytes / 8 MB, rounded up to a power of 2) so a partition's
     working set stays ≤ L2.
7. **Dedup without touching the rows:** per eid partition (parallel), use a small open-addressing table to find keys
   seen more than once, along with their minimum ingest and how many copies share it.
   - For the ~3 % duplicated keys, send `(eid, min_ingest, tie)` only to the person partitions that hold a copy.
   - Each person partition builds a tiny local "dup" table (about 2.5k entries, L1-resident). While scanning its rows,
     it drops any row whose eid is in the table with `ingest ≠ min`, plus all but one of the equal-ingest identical copies.
   - If the minimum copy was invalid, every valid copy has `ingest ≠ min` and is dropped. That's the "no rescue" rule,
     for free.
   - The normal row needs one L1 probe. No second shuffle and no 80 B sort.
8. **Per person partition:**
   - Intern persons locally (dense u16 to u32 IDs).
   - Do a counting sort by local person, then sort each person's ts (avg 30 rows; insertion sort below 32, pdqsort
     above). Or use a single LSD radix sort on `local_person << 32 | ts_off`.
   - Clicks go to the same partition, sorted by `(person, ts, click_id desc)` using the packed click_id.
   - Then run v1's existing session and sliding-window attribution loop **unchanged** (it's already linear and correct).
9. **Aggregation:** per-worker `camp[ncamp+1][ndays][4]` arrays (as in v1), plus user rows. Write Parquet in parallel,
   uncompressed, as now.
10. **Exit:** flush and close the outputs, then `std::process::exit(0)`. Never run destructors or munmap. Peak anonymous
    memory is ≈ 1.0 GB at 20M and ≈ 3 GB at 60M, against 4.1 and 11.5 GB, which removes most Linux page-fault cost and
    the RSS warning.

Budget for this design at 20M, local M5: parse 1.0 core-s, clicks 0.2, dedup 0.15, sessions 0.35, write 0.05,
links 0.03. That's ≈ 1.75 core-s ÷ 6.4 ≈ **0.27 s + ~0.04 s serial ≈ 0.30 s local**. Whether that beats 0.28 s on the
board depends on the calibration ratio (§7). Realistically, this architecture puts us **in the 0.3 to 0.5 s board band
in 12 h**. Leading the board also needs the scorer to be no slower than our M5, or a further SIMD-heavy round.

## 5. Ranked next moves for Method (≤ 5)

Gains are local wall-clock (M5, NCPU=8). The scorer gains are likely larger for moves 1, 3 and 4, which remove
Linux-specific memory costs.

| # | Move | Gain 20M | Gain 60M | Correctness risk | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Skip teardown | −0.10 s | −0.33 s | very low | 15 min |
| 2 | Packed IDs + compact rows + single shuffle + dedup-by-side-stream | −0.65 s | −2.5 s | medium | 3–4 h |
| 3 | SIMD structural scan + SWAR timestamps (fast path, exact fallback) | −0.25 s | −0.9 s | low–medium | 2–3 h |
| 4 | Batched/prefetched lookups + direct product/campaign index + parallel links | −0.15 s | −0.6 s | low | 1–2 h |
| 5 | Memory layout for Linux: arenas, adaptive P, hugepages, input populate | −0.03 s local (−0.1 to −0.3 s scorer) | −0.2 s local (−0.5 s scorer) | low | 1 h |

1. **Skip teardown.** After the last `write_parquet` returns (`ArrowWriter::close` done, `File` dropped), call
   `std::process::exit(0)`. Keep the mid-run `drop(ev_parts)`: forgetting it would add up to 5 GB of footprint at 60M.
   - Verify: "after last mark" falls to < 0.02 s at 20M; fixtures, dev and fuzz stay green; output files are complete
     (the canonical hash is unchanged).
2. **Packed IDs, compact rows, single shuffle, dedup by side stream** (§4.3 items 3, 6, 7, 8). This is the largest
   gain: dedup 0.196 → 0.03, sessions 0.292 → 0.07, parse −0.25 s, peak memory 4.1 → ~1 GB.
   - Risk sits in three places: (a) the order-preserving pack and its fallback mixing (a packed name against a
     fallback name when computing a component's minimum: compare by bytes whenever either side is a fallback); (b) the
     dedup "no rescue" and equal-ingest tie handling; (c) `ingest` as u32 seconds (it must stay exact for whatever
     reaches the comparison: map an unparsable ingest to MAX, as v1 does).
   - Verify: the full fuzz suite (its dedup, links and dialect families target exactly these), the 20M oracle, and
     Eval's new knobs (§6: forced hash collisions, tiny chunks, P=1).
3. **SIMD structural scan and SWAR timestamps, behind a fast path with v1's exact fallback.**
   - A 64 B classifier gives `,` `\n` `"` masks (`std::arch` NEON plus AVX-512BW/AVX2 behind `cfg(target_arch)` and
     *compile-time* `target_feature`, since the Dockerfile builds `cascadelake`), with a portable u64 SWAR path.
   - A line takes the fast path only if it has no quote bit and exactly N−1 commas. Everything else goes through the
     current `split_fields`, so semantics can't diverge on dirty lines.
   - SWAR F1/F2: load 16 + 8 bytes, check the separators with one mask compare, convert digits with a multiply-add.
     The calendar checks stay as they are.
   - Verify: the fuzz dialect and timestamp foci, fixtures, and an amd64-container run (the fallback must be what
     runs under `x86-64-v2` emulation, and the AVX-512 path is only exercised on the scorer, so the SWAR path must be
     bit-identical to it; ask Eval for a forced-SWAR run).
   - Expected: events parse ≈ 4.3 → ~1.5 core-s with move 2, ≈ 0.15–0.20 s wall.
4. **Batched, prefetched lookups; direct product and campaign index; parallel links.**
   - Process rows in groups of 16: compute the packed keys and hashes, `prefetch` the table slots, then probe.
   - Products `p<digits>` index a `Vec<u8>`, with a hash fallback for any other form.
   - Links: parallel parse plus sharded interning (lookups 0.095 → 0.02 s at 20M, 0.32 → 0.07 s at 60M).
   - Verify: the fuzz links focus (chains, upper-case, link-only names, UTF-8) and the 60M consistency diff.
5. **Memory layout for Linux.**
   - Per-worker arenas sized from the file bytes (no Vec doubling or realloc).
   - Adaptive partition count (P ≈ input / 8 MB) so dedup and sort stay in L2 at 60M.
   - `madvise(MADV_HUGEPAGE)` on the arenas and the link table.
   - `MAP_POPULATE`, or a parallel pre-touch, of the input.
   - Verify: Eval's Linux proxy (§6.3); a minor-fault count ≤ 1/4 of v1's.

**Incremental or rewrite?** It's a **staged rewrite of the data path**, keeping v1's validated leaf code: `parse_ts`,
`parse_price`, `split_fields` as the slow path, the session and attribution loop, the output writers. Tuning v1 in
place (moves 1, 4 and 5 without 2) bottoms out around 1.0 to 1.1 s. The 80 B row, two shuffles and slice-compare sort
are structural. A from-scratch rewrite throws away the exact leaf semantics that pass 842 fuzz cases. Suggested
versions, each run through the full gate and submitted if SUBMIT-READY (submissions only ever help):

- **v2 = move 1** plus `--profile full` re-eval: a quick, safe win (~1.45 s) that gives a second calibration point.
- **v3 = moves 2 + 4:** new data layout, the big step (~0.6 to 0.7 s).
- **v4 = move 3:** SIMD (~0.4 s).
- **v5 = move 5** plus tuning against the Linux proxy.

The time budget is about 8 h of Method work plus ~4 × 15 min of eval, which leaves slack for one failed iteration.

## 6. Eval framework gaps (requests for Eval)

1. **Calibration is the gating unknown.** Record v1's board time in `calibration.json` the moment it's submitted, and
   add a second point (v2) quickly: one point can't separate a fixed offset (container start) from a slope. Fit
   `board = a + b·local`, not a pure ratio.
2. **Thread efficiency is misleading on this host (5P + 10E).** NCPU=8 means 5 P-cores + 3 E-cores, so 0.675 is
   ≈ 98 % of what's achievable. Report the efficiency against a P-core-equivalent ceiling (≈ 6.4), or add NCPU=5
   (all P-cores) as a scaling point. Also add per-phase speedup to the scaling table (the data is in
   `per_ncpu_detail` already), so a serial phase shows up directly.
3. **Add a Linux scorer proxy (the biggest missing measurement).** Run 20M in the arm64 Linux container with the data
   in a **named Docker volume** (ext4 inside the VM, page-cache backed), not the virtiofs bind mount. The 1.49× ratio
   today is virtiofs noise. This exposes what the M5/macOS numbers hide:
   - 4 KB-page first-touch faults (measured ≈ 0.1 s/GB in this VM);
   - THP behaviour (the VM has `always`; the scorer likely has `madvise`, so also run with THP set to `madvise`
     inside a privileged container if possible);
   - exit teardown.

   Report `minflt` and `majflt` from `/usr/bin/time -v` (or `getrusage`) there.
4. **Use instructions per input row as a primary perf signal.** v1's instructions are 91.8 G ± 0.1 %, far less noisy
   than wall time (CV 2 %), and roughly ISA-portable in ratio. Report instructions/row, cycles/row and IPC per dataset,
   and IPC per size. The 60M IPC drop (2.04 → 1.75) is the cleanest superlinearity diagnostic we have. Instructions
   also make A/B on sub-0.4 s versions meaningful when wall noise ≈ the gain.
5. **Get more samples once runs are fast.** At < 0.5 s, use ≥ 11 runs, report min and median, keep interleaving A/B,
   and record the P/E split if possible. v1's 20M samples are already bimodal (1.547 against 1.60).
6. **Correctness tests for the classes of bug the next versions can introduce.** These need small env knobs from Method:
   - **Forced hash collisions:** something like `SESSIONS_DEBUG_HASH_BITS=4` truncates every internal hash, so that
     equality fallbacks (eid dedup, person keys, the link table) run constantly. Run the whole fuzz suite under it.
   - **Tiny chunks** (`SESSIONS_DEBUG_CHUNK=97`): exercises line splits at chunk boundaries and per-chunk state.
   - **Partition count extremes** (`P=1`, `P=4096`).
   - **Forced fallback paths:** turn off the SIMD fast path, turn off the packed-ID fast path, and require bit-identical
     canonical output against the default. This is the guard for move 3, because the AVX-512 code path only runs on
     the scorer; an x86 build with AVX2 under emulation would at least cover x86 SIMD.
   - **Fuzz ID-shape cases:** IDs of exactly 10 and 11 chars (the pack boundary), `_` and uppercase mixed with lower
     inside one person (packed against fallback minimum), and IDs that differ only past char 10.
   - **A second 20M oracle seed** with a different window (ideally one including Feb 29). Whales and shared devices
     only appear at full size, and seed-42 is the only full-size oracle. It's a one-off ~4 min reference run.
7. **Per-phase CPU time.** Ask Method to append `cpu=X.XXs` (`getrusage(RUSAGE_SELF)`) to each stderr mark, keeping
   the format. Eval can then report per-phase utilisation, which separates "serial" from "slow" without the
   NCPU sweep.
8. **Cold cache.** Measure one cold-cache 20M run (`purge` on macOS) to quantify read cost, in case the scorer's first
   of 3 runs is cold. It's informational, since the score is a median of 3.

## 7. Confidence and open questions

- **High confidence:** v1 is CPU-work-bound, not parallelism-bound (from utilisation, per-phase speedup and user time).
  The field split and link lookup are the two biggest per-row costs (single-thread probe ablation, cumulative). Compact
  rows alone won't get us there (the probe hit 0.50 s against 0.69 s). Teardown is serial, at 0.11 s and 0.35 s.
- **Medium confidence:** the ≈ 0.30 to 0.40 s local endpoint for the full architecture. It's extrapolated from probes,
  not built, and it assumes ~6.4 effective cores. Per-move gains overlap, so don't sum them blindly.
- **Low confidence: the local→scorer ratio.** This is the biggest open question, and it decides whether "beat 0.28 s"
  is reachable at all. Submit v1.
- **Open questions:**
  1. Does the board time include `docker run` start? If it includes ~0.1 to 0.3 s of container start, leaders are
     near the I/O floor, and our optimizations past ~0.3 s local stop mattering.
  2. Is the scorer's page cache warm (median of 3 suggests yes)?
  3. THP mode on the scorer: `always` or `madvise`. We should `madvise` explicitly either way.
  4. Is the vCPU count 8 physical cores, or 4 cores with HT? It changes the value of the compute-bound moves (2, 3, 4)
     against the memory-bound ones (5).
  5. The ID formats (≤ 9 chars, `[0-9a-z]`) come from the generator. The final dataset uses the same generator at
     60M, and the fallback path keeps us exact if that ever changes.

## Appendix: probe methods (scratchpad only, machine idle, load avg ~2)

- `floor` (Rust, rayon 8 threads, mmap of seed-42 events + clicks, 1.90 GB, warm cache): touch 1 byte per 4 KB;
  sum u64; memchr `\n` count; memchr2 `,`/`\n` count; split lines plus xxh3 of the first field; `fs::read` per file.
