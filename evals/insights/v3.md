**Verdict: SUBMIT-READY**, and I agree. Gates A, B and C pass:

- **Submit v3 now.** Its board time turns that 4× bracket into one number and tells us how deep v4 must go (§3).

## 2. What went well

- **Every v3 move delivered.** −1,666 instructions per line against v2, and IPC rose from 1.95 to 3.17, so packed
  keys and compact rows removed cache misses as well as instructions.
- **Scaling is now linear in size:** 29.0 ns/event at 20M and 30.7 at 60M. Overall 60M/20M = ×3.16 time for
  ×2.98 events. v1 was about 25% superlinear.
- **Linux is now faster than macOS native** (0.545 vs 0.624 s). mimalloc plus THP took faults to 16k (from 967k
  with glibc) and the exit tail to 10 ms. The fault and exit costs I flagged for the scorer in v2 are gone.
- **Memory:** a 5.62 GB footprint at 60M, with 18 GB of headroom under the 24 GB cap.
- **The parse phases scale:** events parse 6.97× and sessions 6.64× at 8 threads, against 6.4 P-core
  equivalents. That's at the hardware ceiling.

## 3. What failed, regressed, or is misleading

- **Calibration is still empty** (`evals/submissions.json`). Every absolute claim about the board is a model.
- **Linux A/B was unavailable.** The 60 s contamination guard postponed it, because another thread's `rustc` was
  at 100% CPU.
  - The native 20M and 60M runs were flagged noisy (CV 0.042 and 0.038). `rustc` at 100% and `python` at 360%
    were running during them.
  - The verdict holds anyway (Δ 0.98 s vs 2 sd 0.21 s), but per-phase numbers carry about ±4%.
- **The native exit tail is a macOS artifact:** 0.060 s at 20M (10% of wall) and 0.158 s at 60M, against 0.010 s
  on Linux. Native A/B timing over-weights any change that touches RSS or teardown. Rank by Linux wall, or by
  native "wall − tail".
- **The poorly scaling phases now take 29% of native wall but 15% of CPU** (Amdahl):

  | Phase | Wall at 20M | Speedup at 8 threads |
  | --- | ---: | ---: |
  | lookups / links | 0.054 s | 2.44× |
  | dedup | 0.033 s | 4.09× |
  | written | 0.036 s | 3.36× |
  | macOS exit tail | 0.060 s | – |

## 4. Fast-path coverage (measured)

## 5. Bottleneck model, re-checked with measured numbers

These are per-phase instructions per event line. I split v3's measured 55.9 G by each phase's share of measured CPU,
assuming flat IPC. Eval doesn't measure instructions per phase yet (§7).

| Phase | v3 measured instr/line | My estimate | Budget | Wall 20M → 60M (native) | 60M/20M | Gap closed by |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| Lookups + links | **100** | 40 | 10 | **0.054 → 0.216 s**, parallelism 2.9 → 3.5 | **×4.0** | G10 parallel links |
| Events parse | 1,441 | 1,400 | 125 | 0.291 → 0.861 s | ×2.96 | G1, G3, G2 emit |
| Clicks parse | 221 | 330 | 25 | 0.047 → 0.136 s | ×2.9 | G1, G4 |
| Dedup | 153 | 185 | 15 | 0.033 → **0.137 s** | **×4.2** | G5, G6 (P scaling) |
| Sessions + attribution | 550 | 600 | 70 | 0.110 → **0.394 s** | **×3.6** | G2, G6 |
| Output | 134 | 100 | 5 | 0.036 → 0.078 s (Linux 0.026) | ×2.2 | G9, parallel |
| **Total** | **2,598** | ≈ 2,700 | ≈ 250 | 0.624 → 1.970 s | ×3.16 | |

What changed in the model:
1. **The estimates held for events, dedup and sessions (within 10%).** Clicks are cheaper than I thought.
   Links are 2.5× costlier.
2. **Lookups and links are the worst phase relative to budget**: 10× over on instructions, and the phase barely
   parallelises.
   - At 60M it's **0.216 s, 11% of the final's wall**. That's more than dedup and output combined.
   - On a scorer with fewer effective cores, a phase at parallelism 3 loses less than the others. But it's still
     ≈ 0.1–0.2 s at 60M.
   - The serial parts are the union-find loop over all pairs, the "best ID" loop, and `binary_search` for every
     key in every pair.
3. **Superlinear growth at 60M is concentrated in dedup (×4.2) and sessions (×3.6), with P fixed at 256.**
   - A 60M partition holds ≈ 250k rows. Its dedup table is (2 × 250k → 512k slots × 16 B) = 8 MB, and its `PEv`
     buffer is 6 MB. Both are far past L2.
   - **Scaling P with input belongs in the same commit as compact rows (G2 + G6)**, because the final is 60M.
4. **Budget:** ≈ 250 instructions per line is still the target if the scorer is 4 cores × 2 HT. If it is 8 real
   cores, the target is ≈ 500.
   - Either way v3 is 5–10× above it.
   - G1 + G2 + G3 + G10 (≈ −1,600 [est.]) bring v3 to ≈ 1,000 per line, roughly 2.5× fewer instructions.
   - Beating 0.28 s also needs G4–G9.
   - The calibration point decides whether the incremental path is enough.

## 7. Eval framework gaps

1. **Calibration (blocking every absolute claim):** submit v3, then record it in `submissions.json` and
   `make eval-calibrate`.
2. **Linux 20M as the primary timing.** Native wall includes a macOS-only exit tail of 60–160 ms. Rank Linux first,
   or report native "wall − tail".
   - The contamination guard did its job: it postponed the Linux A/B, which is why it is missing.
   - The orchestrator should hold builds off the machine while Eval holds the lock. `rustc` was at 100% during the
     native runs too.
3. **Per-phase instructions and cycles.** Read the counters at every `lap`, so a move's gain can be attributed
   without assuming flat IPC.
4. **Fast-path coverage counter:** under a debug stats flag, count lines by path (fast / fallback / malformed) per
   input kind. That lets v4's G1 be verified on real data, not just on unit tests.
5. **Partition skew:** max/mean rows per person partition, and the wall of the slowest `process_part`, at 20M and
   60M. That checks G6.
6. **Superlinearity per phase:** a 60M/20M ratio per phase in the report, like the table in §5. It catches cache
   cliffs that the overall ×3.16 hides.

## Addendum: re-anchored on the first board point (v3 = 1.9424 s, "wait 141 ms")

**The point:**
- v3 `29b64bf` scored 1.9424 s on the practice board, which is **3.10×** our local 20M native median (0.626 s) and
  **3.56×** the local Linux container (0.545 s).
- v3 executes 55.9 G instructions on seed-42, which has 21.51M event lines.
- The starter README confirms the box: *"8 vCPUs, hyperthreads of an Intel Xeon"* on GCP n2 (Cascade Lake or newer).
  That's 4 cores × 2 HT, the slow end of my bracket.

### A. What "wait 141 ms" is

**It isn't defined anywhere we have.**
- `../context/rules.md` is identical to `docs/rules.md` apart from whitespace. It says only that the run is timed.
- The starter README says *"Build time is never timed; only the run is timed."*
- `scripts/docker_run.sh` is a plain `docker run --rm` with the scorer's flags. It has no timer and no wait step.

**Two readings, and what each implies:**

| Reading | Effective rate for v3 | Instructions per line allowed for 0.28 s |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| **(a) Excluded:** container start or queueing, reported for information | 55.9 G / 1.942 s = **28.8 G instr/s** | 0.28 × 28.8 G / 21.5M ≈ **375** |
| **(b) Included:** a fixed cost inside the 1.9424 | 55.9 G / 1.801 s = **31.0 G instr/s** | (0.28 − 0.141) × 31.0 G / 21.5M ≈ **200** |

**What points toward (b):**
- Summed v3 phase marks × 3.10 come to 1.77 s, which leaves ≈ 0.17 s of the board time unexplained. That's about
  141 ms plus exit.
- Our own `docker run` overhead on dev is +0.165 s (Eval's container check).
- Neither is proof: the 3.10× ratio comes from the same board point, so the first argument is partly circular.

**What points toward (a):**
- Under (b), the leaders would be doing 1.8 GB in ≈ 0.14 s of compute on 4 cores, ≈ 110 instr/line. That's close
  to a pure SIMD scan with almost no work per row, which I find implausible for this spec.
- About 12 teams cluster at 0.28–0.31 s. That fits a common cost floor at least as well as a common fixed cost.

**It doesn't change the move order**, because every move cuts per-line instructions and nothing we control
touches the 141 ms. It does change how far we have to go: (a) means 2.7× beyond moves 1–4, and (b) means 5×.

**How to settle it, without spending a submission:**
- Ask the user to read the track page's definition of "wait".
- Or have the user check whether the leaders' entries show a similar wait. If a 0.28 s entry shows about 140 ms of
  wait, then (b) is unlikely, since it would leave about 0.14 s of compute.
- Otherwise the next submission settles it. If wait stays about 141 ms while the score moves, (a) is the more
  likely reading.

### B. The budget, re-anchored

- **Use about 375 instructions per event line, all phases, as the 0.28 s target**, at the measured ≈ 29 G instr/s.
  The ≈ 250 from §1 of `v4-design.md` was the pessimistic 25 G/s model.
- Under reading (b) the target is ≈ 200, which is close to my original 250.
- Board-seconds rule of thumb at 20M: **100 instr/line ≈ 2.15 G instructions ≈ 0.075 board s** for a phase that
  parallelises at 8. A phase stuck at parallelism p costs about 8/p times that.
- At 60M the final has 3× the lines, so multiply every saving by ≈ 3. Dedup and sessions save more than that,
  because they grow superlinearly.

**Will moves 1–4 land near 1.0–1.1 s on the board? Yes, or a little better.**

| Method | v3 | After moves 1–4 | Board |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Local wall × 3.10 | 0.626 s | ≈ 0.35 s | ≈ 1.08 s |
| Instructions at 28.8 G/s | 2,598 instr/line | ≈ 1,000 | **≈ 0.75 s** (plus links' parallelism tail ≈ 0.05 s) → **≈ 0.8 s** |

**Expect 0.8–1.1 s.** The board tracks instructions more closely than local wall does. The M5's IPC of 3.17 hides
instruction costs that a Cascade Lake pays for. Its local wall also includes a macOS-only exit tail of 60 ms, which
×3.1 would add a fake 0.19 s.

**What 0.28 s needs beyond moves 1–4** (≈ 1,000 → ≈ 375 instructions/line, or ≈ 200 under reading (b)):

| Phase | After moves 1–4 [est.] | Needed | How |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Events parse | ≈ 550 | 150 | Line-at-a-time field extraction with no per-bit type branch. Decode straight from bitmasks (SWAR product, price and type). Drop the 56 B `PendEv` copy per row: keep only (slot, row index) in the prefetch ring. AVX2 classifier behind the startup self-test. |
| Sessions + attribution | ≈ 250 | 60 | **Explorer 3's kernel** (D below) |
| Output | 134 (unchanged) | 10 | **Parquet writer cost:** 134 instr/line is ≈ 1,280 instructions per user_daily row (2.25M rows) |
| Clicks | ≈ 120 | 30 | G1 + G3 applied to clicks, Ck at 24 B |
| Dedup | ≈ 80 | 20 | 16 B entries, an L2-sized table per partition |
| Links | ≈ 30 at parallelism 7 | 10 | G10, then prefetched hash dense-indexing |
| **Total** | **≈ 1,000** | **≈ 280–375** | |

### D. Explorer 3 (Grok + Gemini): scope and acceptance

Explorer 2 owns the sort. Explorer 3 owns everything after the rows are sorted, plus the output. No overlap.

1. **Sessions and attribution kernel, ≤ 60 instructions per surviving event row.** Input: one partition's rows,
   sorted by (local person, ts_off), as 16 B rows; and clicks sorted by (local person, ts, click_id desc).
   - It covers the gap test, per-session revenue and purchase flag, per-day user accumulation and top category,
     the credited click, and assists.
   - Known costs to remove:
     - the per-session `for &c in &active.list` assist loop: make it O(1) amortised per click with a per-campaign
       "last counted session" stamp;
     - `flush_user` scanning all `ncat` categories per (person, day): track the touched categories;
     - scattered `acc.camp` writes: a campaign × day matrix per worker is fine, but index it `day * (ncamp + 1) + c`
       for locality.
   - Acceptance:
     - bit-identical `user_daily` and `campaign_daily` on dev, seed-42 and seed-43;
     - the v3-review `whale` case, and fuzz under every knob;
     - instructions per row measured with the counters on seed-42 partitions dumped to disk, whale partition
       included.
2. **Parquet output cost.** Measure the instructions per output row of the current writer (`WriterProperties`
   defaults: dictionary on, statistics on), then the cheapest configuration the checker accepts:
   - dictionary off, statistics off, plain encoding, a single large row group, uncompressed;
   - optionally a minimal hand-rolled writer for the two fixed schemas.
   - Target ≤ 100 instructions per output row, which is ≈ 10 per input line.
   - Acceptance: Eval's checker and `make check-dev` pass, and `pyarrow` reads the files with `day` as `DATE`.
3. **Fused-stage microbenchmark at ≈ 300 instructions per event line (clean-room).** A standalone binary over
   seed-42 that runs the stages cumulatively and reports instructions per line at each step, as in v1 §4.2 but with
   v4's design:
   1. classify, then extract fields line by line;
   2. pack the IDs;
   3. decode ts and ingest;
   4. type, product and price;
   5. prefetched link probe;
   6. emit two 16 B streams.
   - It tells Method which stage misses its line in the budget table (B), and by how much, before Method rewrites
     that stage.
   - Not exact on its own; it isn't shipped.
